[x_498f7161c2217090]
How Do We Stop on a Vowelled Letter at the End of a Word?

[s057_01]
When reading the Quran, we usually stop at the ends of verses, at stopping marks, or when we need to breathe. These stops can only be at the ends of words.

[s057_02]
We do not stop by pronouncing fathah, kasrah or dammah. If the final letter is not already vowel-free—with sukoon or as a lengthening letter—its vowel is omitted and it is pronounced as though it had sukoon.

[s057_03]
For tanween, the rule is as follows: With double fathah written alongside alif, one fathah is removed. The letter is read with a single fathah, lengthened for two counts.

[s058_01]
When stopping on a hamzah with double fathah written without alif, it is also lengthened for two counts, as though an alif were beside it.

[s058_02]
On letters with double kasrah or double dammah, we stop with sukoon.
